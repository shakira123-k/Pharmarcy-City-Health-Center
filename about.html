Developing a mobile-based health digital platform for improved patient monitoring

A Makerere University Business School project for Pharmacy City Health Center, Nakawa Division, Kampala District.

The problem

Pharmacy City Health Center tracks outpatients and discharged patients with paper follow-up cards and record books. Patients must travel to the clinic to have vitals checked, files get misplaced or damaged, and clinicians cannot see a patient’s condition between visits. That delays treatment, increases missed follow-ups and crowds the outpatient department.

The goal

Design and develop a mobile-based health digital platform that supports continuous outpatient tracking and real-time patient monitoring.

Project objectives

Study

Analyse how patients are tracked and monitored at the health centre today.

Model

Identify user and technical requirements for the platform.

Build

Develop a working prototype for patients and clinicians.

Test

Evaluate functionality, usability and performance with real users.

Who benefits

No more paper errors, early warning of deterioration through automatic threshold alerts, and fewer crowded clinic days because non-critical follow-ups are handled remotely.

Lower travel costs, better medicine adherence through reminders, improved health literacy and direct access to a clinician.

Hands-on experience in full-stack development, database design, Agile and Design Science methods, and secure system design.

How it is built

Front end

HTML5, CSS3 and JavaScript. Mobile-first layout with a bottom tab bar for patients.

Prototype data

This demo keeps its data in the browser (localStorage), so no server is needed to try it.

Planned back end

PHP with MySQL in third normal form: users, patients, vitals, alerts, medications and messages.

Planned security

Hashed passwords, role-based access, prepared statements and escaped output in the server version.

Help and FAQ

How do I record my vitals?

Log in, open Log vitals, type the five numbers from your devices and press Save reading. The safety check tells you how the numbers look before you save.

What do the colours mean?

Green means stable. Amber means your clinician will review the reading. Red means urgent: contact the clinic straight away, because the system has already alerted them.

Who can see my readings?

Only you and the clinicians at Pharmacy City Health Center. Passwords are hashed and the patient and clinician areas are separated by role. This demo stores its data only in your own browser.

I forgot to take my medicine. What now?

Open the medicine list and message your clinician for advice. Do not double a dose unless they tell you to.

How do clinicians know a reading is unsafe?

Every reading is compared with safe limits for blood pressure, heart rate, temperature and glucose. Unsafe readings create an alert that appears on the triage dashboard within 10 seconds.

Is this for emergencies?

No. If you feel very unwell, go to the nearest health facility or call your local emergency line. This platform supports follow-up care; it does not replace emergency care.
